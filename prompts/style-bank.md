STYLE BANK AUTHORING GUIDE
=============================================================================

WHAT THIS IS:
Each line below is a standalone visual style directive. Three are randomly
selected per generation run — one per image concept. The directive tells
Claude how to ART-DIRECT the image: the medium, technique, rendering
approach, lighting, texture, and color treatment. The fund thesis (provided
by the user) determines WHAT the image depicts. The style directive
determines HOW it looks.

FORMAT:
- One style per line, plain text, no bullets or numbering
- No trailing periods
- Typically 8–20 words

STRUCTURE — follow this formula:
  [medium / technique / genre], [specific visual detail], [second detail or mood]

Examples of GOOD entries:
  cyanotype print, deep Prussian blue with white silhouettes
  sports photography, 35mm film grain, peak action freeze frame
  noir cinema still, harsh venetian blind shadows across a subject
  chrome reflection, mirror-polished metal warping its surroundings
  Kodachrome 64 slide film, saturated reds and deep blacks with warm cast

WHAT MAKES AN ENTRY 11/10:
1. MEDIUM-FIRST — Lead with the art form, photographic process, rendering
   technique, or artistic movement. This is the anchor.
2. SPECIFIC ENOUGH TO PICTURE — "oil painting" is too vague.
   "oil painting on linen, visible brushstrokes, Renaissance chiaroscuro
   lighting" is right. You should be able to close your eyes and see it.
3. SUBJECT-AGNOSTIC — The style must work with ANY subject: a bubble, a
   ship, a skyscraper, a stack of cash. If the style only makes sense for
   one type of subject, it's too narrow.
4. DESCRIBES HOW, NOT WHAT — The directive controls rendering, texture,
   lighting, and color. It does NOT introduce objects, scenes, or subjects.
   If the style mentions specific objects (e.g. "platinum bands"), treat
   them as material/textural references, not literal things to depict.
5. TASTEFUL AND HIGH-END — Think gallery, editorial, cinematic. Every
   entry should produce a result that looks like it belongs on a museum
   wall, in a luxury magazine, or in a film. Nothing gimmicky, kitschy,
   or amateur.
6. NO DUPLICATE TERRITORY — Before adding, scan existing entries to ensure
   you're not covering ground that's already well-represented.

WHAT TO AVOID:
- Styles that prescribe specific subjects or scenes
  BAD:  "three platinum bands floating on black velvet"
  GOOD: "precision-milled platinum finish, machined metal sheen on black velvet"
- Text, logos, readable typography, or UI elements as the subject
- Anything requiring detailed human faces or bare hands (Midjourney weakness)
- Overly niche styles that only work with one type of image
- Entries shorter than 6 words (too vague) or longer than 25 words (too prescriptive)

SPECIAL CATEGORY — HEDGE FUND LOGO MARKS:
These entries specifically direct Claude to create a logo/emblem/crest for
the fund. They describe the RENDERING TREATMENT of the mark (material,
finish, lighting), NOT the symbol itself — the fund thesis drives that.
  GOOD: "3D hedge fund logo mark, chrome beveled emblem with environment reflections on matte black"
  BAD:  "hedge fund logo with falcon and compass on shield" (prescribes the symbol)

CATEGORIES TO DRAW FROM:
Photography (studio, street, macro, aerial, underwater, infrared, film stocks)
Painting (oil, acrylic, watercolor, gouache, encaustic, fresco)
Print (screen print, risograph, linocut, etching, cyanotype, mezzotint)
Drawing (pencil, charcoal, ink, ballpoint, scratchboard)
Sculpture & 3D (bronze, marble, glass, ceramic, chrome, resin)
Digital (octane render, data visualization, generative, glassmorphism)
Film & Cinema (specific cinematographers, color grades, lens effects)
Fine Art Movements (specific artists' signature styles)
Material Studies (metal, glass, stone, fabric, liquid)
Collage & Mixed Media (torn paper, found objects, photographic transfer)
Street Art (graffiti, stencil, wheat paste, spray paint)
